Fix the loader that never finished; draw the Jet d'Eau properly; make the demos usable on a phone

The loading animation never stopped. cfLoadStop was only called when the modal was
closed, so nothing cleared the spinner when the iframe finished — every interactive
tool sat behind a permanent animation and could only be reached by opening it in a
new tab. The frame's load event now clears it, with an eight-second backstop so a
tool that stalls shows itself anyway rather than trapping the loader again. Fixed on
all three pages that carry the modal.

The loading mark is now actually the Jet d'Eau. It was a gold vertical line with no
city and no water. It is now the Rade: building silhouettes seated on the arc of the
bay rather than on a flat line, which is the difference between a skyline and a bar
chart, with St-Pierre's twin towers and spire above the old town, and a fountain in
water blue — a jet that climbs, a plume that feathers open behind it, and spray that
falls back. The reduced-motion path shows it formed rather than frozen mid-climb.

ARGUS 3D camera. It broke off to watch the Moon and then Mars go past, which reads
as losing the shot, and it pulled back to a wide reveal from a third of the way
through the impact hold, so the impact was already receding as it happened. The
fly-past pan is now capped at an 18% lean of the look direction and only fires when a
body genuinely fills the frame; the camera settles onto the target from 52% of the
coast rather than 72%; the impact hold stays close until 66% before easing out; and
the aftermath framing no longer retreats far enough to make the body a dot. The
demo's description was rewritten to say what the camera now does.

Phones. The six demo pages ran to two to five screens of which the canvas was one —
the rest was the lede and the caveat notes you scroll past before touching anything.
The prose is not removed, because several of those notes are the reason the page is
honest, but on a small screen the lede clamps to four lines and each note folds into
a labelled disclosure, and the panel holding the visual is promoted to the top of the
grid. Applied by script, so nothing is hidden from a reader without JavaScript, and
desktop is untouched.

One Geneva everywhere; the billboard built into the hero; the camera finally holds the impact

Five independent design reviews were run on the proposed landing-page split
(a billboard front door with the CV demoted to an About page). Four returned
"modified", one "against", none endorsed the split: the root URL is literally
/CV/, the page's search presence lives on its text, and a gateway screen costs
a click before any evidence. What survived of the idea is built here instead -
the billboard is now IN the hero: eight wayfinding tiles with one-line
descriptions and counts (Projects 8, Crisis studies 3, Publications 28,
Research Lab 16), contact on the first screen, and a two-button audience fork
- "Hiring?" and "Here from a citation?" - above the tailoring chips. Under the
research lens the sidebar now sells the work, not the job hunt: the
availability pill and CV button give way to "Publications - 28 works".

The Geneva skyline is now one shared implementation on every first-party page.
It had been painted per-column on the home page only, which is why the art
slammed into the sidebar edge and the CV button hung below the horizon - the
reported jank. The procedural scene (Saleve and Jura, St-Pierre, the shore
city, Lac Leman, the Jet d'Eau with its spray) moved to assets/chrome.js and
draws once per page as a fixed full-viewport layer behind a now-translucent
sidebar, animated on the home page, drawn once everywhere else, faded at its
top edge so it never fights the text. 26 subpages carry it now - publications,
papers, case studies, coverpages, the map. Every page has a light/dark toggle:
pages without their own get a floating one. In-character product demos (FRIS,
Morris Anderson, the consoles) are deliberately excluded - a Geneva sidebar
inside a product fiction would break the product.

The ARGUS 3D camera bug is actually fixed this time, and the cause was worth
finding: the target closes at 70 u/s while the camera holds a 26-72 u
standoff, and a plain world-space lerp lags a mover of that speed by up to
~30 u - so the asteroid physically overran the camera during every impact
hold, at any speed. The camera now rides the body: the focus's own
frame-to-frame displacement is applied 1:1 and the lerp smooths only the
cinematic offsets. The impact view also derives its directions from each
shot's approach axis (they were fixed world constants, so half the shots
watched the wrong face), and the hold tracks the live scar rather than the
frozen prediction. Measured across the hold: the camera never crosses the
target plane and settles to 0.3 degrees off the body.

The Jet d'Eau loading mark now shows when apps open from the portfolio page -
it was on three hub pages but not the one page where every platform actually
launches. Same art, same clear-on-load wiring.

Wording: "~20,000 lines of JavaScript, authored by me" read as protesting.
It is now "a single-author codebase of ~20,000 lines of JavaScript" - the
same fact, stated as a property of the code.

Verified: 27 pages rendered at 390 and 1440 in a real browser - canvas
present, toggle present, no horizontal overflow, zero page errors, 54/54
clean - plus visual inspection of the home page (both sizes), the tiles, the
publications hub, a paper page, and the research-lens sidebar swap.
